About 60 s, 7 clicks from opening the page to a finished five-rep set on the simulator

Problem statement

A judge opens a GitHub link, runs one command, and has about a minute of attention. They need to see that Rehabify does more than count: every rep is checked for range, pace and hold, and the patient feels it through the brace. Today the page opens on a setup form, the simulated leg moves by itself, and good and bad reps only differ if you find a dropdown in the header.

Screen by screen

All steps live on one page. The live leg stays in place throughout; the panel beside it (below it on a phone) changes with the step, so the visitor never loses the object that matters.

0. Opening the page

Why it exists: the first impression is the claim. The live leg is already on screen, resting, with the brace connection shown plainly. The panel offers one action: set your rest position. While the event stream connects, the leg is drawn in a muted resting state rather than a spinner.

1. Set rest position

Why: every angle is measured from rest, so this step is the foundation and must feel quick. Instruction: sit tall, thigh supported, foot relaxed. A 2-second ring fills around the knee while the visitor holds still. Success: "Rest position set." Failure (leg moved): "You moved a little. Let's try that again," with the same button. Nothing else on the page asks for attention until this is done.

2. Today's plan

Why: the targets come from the physiotherapist, so the patient confirms rather than configures. The plan reads as a sentence: "Straighten to 50°, hold for 2 seconds, 5 times." Adjust opens the six fields inline; the plan picker sits inside it. Training-data labelling lives behind a separate "Record training data" disclosure, out of the judge's path. Primary action: Start set.

3. Do the reps

Why: this is the moment Rehabify is felt. The leg rises; the target zone on its arc glows as the shin enters it; the brace clicks; a hold arc fills; a double click marks the hold. Rep progress shows as segments of a ring, one per rep, so the set's progress is readable at a glance. On the simulator, three buttons perform one rep each: "Good rep", "Stop short", "Rush it". The leg rests between presses, so the judge controls the pace of the demo.

4. Rep feedback

Why: understanding comes after the movement, never during it. When a rep ends, its ring segment fills in a colour that matches the result and a short line appears: "Rep 3: full range, steady, held 2.4 s." A short rep reads "Rep 4: 41° of your 50°. Ease a little further next time." A rushed rep reads "Rep 5: that one was quick. Take about 2 seconds on the way up." Range, pace and hold are shown as three plain checks, then the detail table below.

5. Set complete

Why: closure and progress. The ring completes, the brace buzzes, and the summary leads with what went well, then what to work on, then the comparison with the previous set ("6° more range than Tuesday"). Actions: Start another set, and the progress chart below for the longer view.

Interaction specifications

  • Live leg updates at 25 frames per second from the stream, with no easing on the angle itself: it must feel attached to the body.
  • Haptic events (target, hold, set done) also show as a visual pulse on the knee at the same instant, so the vibration is visible on a video.
  • Panel changes between steps cross-fade and shift by a few pixels within 250 ms; no motion when the visitor prefers reduced motion.
  • Keyboard: every action reachable by Tab with a visible focus ring; on the simulator, keys 1, 2, 3 perform a good, short or rushed rep; Space starts and stops a set.
  • Rep results are announced to screen readers through a polite live region: "Rep 3 of 5, good: full range, steady, held 2.4 seconds."

Success criteria

  • A first-time visitor finishes a five-rep set on the simulator without reading anything but the page.
  • After one short and one rushed rep, the visitor can say what each looked like and why it was flagged.
  • Every state in the table above can be reached and reads correctly at 375 px, 768 px and desktop, light and dark.
